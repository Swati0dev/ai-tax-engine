# Old vs New Tax Regime (FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27)

## Summary
India now has two tax regimes: the **New Tax Regime** (which is the default) and the **Old Tax Regime** (which is optional). The New Regime offers lower tax rates but restricts most traditional deductions (like 80C, 80D, HRA). The Old Regime has higher rates but allows you to claim all your deductions.

## Details

### 1. New Tax Regime (Default)
For FY 2025-26, the New Tax Regime is the default. It includes a standard deduction of ₹75,000 for salaried individuals.
- **Tax Rebate:** Income up to ₹12 lakh is effectively tax-free due to the Section 87A rebate (as updated in Finance Bill 2026).
- **Tax Slabs:**
  - Up to ₹4,00,000: Nil
  - ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000: 5%
  - ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000: 10%
  - ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000: 15%
  - ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000: 20%
  - ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000: 25%
  - Above ₹24,00,000: 30%

### 2. Old Tax Regime (Optional)
This allows taxpayers to claim traditional deductions like **Section 80C** (up to ₹1.5L for LIC, PPF, ELSS), **Section 80D** (Health Insurance), and **Section 24(b)** (Home Loan Interest). You have to manually opt-in for this regime when filing your return.

### 💡 Real Life Example
> मान लो Priya एक salaried employee है और सालाना ₹11.5 लाख कमाती है।
> अगर वो **New Regime** चुनती है, तो उसे कोई भी deduction (LIC, Rent etc.) दिखाने की ज़रूरत नहीं है। ₹12 लाख तक की limit होने के कारण उसका टैक्स **Zero** होगा! टेंशन खत्म। ✅
> लेकिन अगर Priya का पैकेज ₹18 लाख है और उसने बहुत सारा Home Loan, LIC और Health Insurance ले रखा है, तो उसे कैलकुलेट करना पड़ेगा कि **Old Regime** में सारे deductions क्लेम करने के बाद टैक्स कम बन रहा है या New Regime के lower rates में।
